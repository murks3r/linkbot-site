Typography review gallery

This page exists to be argued with. It renders the type in the contexts it will actually meet — long descriptions, dense listings, forms, figures, two languages, three viewports, two themes — and it labels what is wrong as clearly as what works. A passing technical gate is not design approval. The owner has rejected the current website's typography, spacing and colour; nothing here should be read as a claim that this replaces that judgement.

How to review the viewports: the two panels below are real 320 px and 390 px containers, driven by CSS container queries, so they reflow independently of this window. Resize this window for 1440 px. Switch theme with the header control; switch listing density to see whether the face survives 15 px.

1 · Glyph spotlight — the characters that decide it

@ & numerals, punctuation, ambiguity

Read this section first. Two of the three flagged glyphs were redrawn in this revision: @ was two concentric rings — a bullseye — and is now a ring with an open inner bowl and a right-hand bar; ? was a flat hook and is now a round bowl with a near-vertical tail. & is recognizable at display sizes but idiosyncratic — at 12–14 px it can read as a figure eight, and it is the one glyph this review still recommends redrawing. The ambiguity card is what matters for a recruiting product full of codes and versions: 1 l I separate by width and by context, 0 O by proportion.

4 · Full job description — the long-text test

Senior Backend Engineer

About the role

Linkbot is a permissioned matching layer for recruiting. The holder keeps a private, versioned record; an employer requests one bounded signal; the holder decides; the disclosure is receipted and revocable for future use. Nothing is published, nothing is scraped, and there is no public profile URL. Requirements render as met, unknown or unmet — an unknown requirement is shortlisted for verification and is never displayed as verified. Employer access is membership- and role-bound, with its own workspace and its own receipts.

The team runs the request path end to end. You will work on the disclosure service, the receipt ledger and the policy-version checks that decide whether a request is authorised at all. Expect to read more specifications than tickets, and to write the specification when one is missing.

Requirements

  • Five or more years building backend services in Go, Java or Rust
  • Comfortable with PostgreSQL: indexes, query plans, migrations at scale
  • Experience with Kubernetes in production, not only in a training cluster
  • Written English at specification level; German is useful, not required

9 · Labelled alternatives

Three decisions, and what each one costs

A1 · Dots — square (current) vs round

ij öü · ! ? : ;

ij öü · ! ? : ;

Current: square dots, the mark's inscribed square, built by one helper. Alternative, rendered above: the same family with the switch flipped (glyphset.DOT_SHAPE = "round"), built to fonts/alternatives/. Cost of changing: one line, and the family loses its loudest identifying feature — which is the whole argument for keeping it.

A2 · Display contrast — 0.60 (current) vs 0.95

Notarial Record

Current horizontal/vertical stroke ratio is 0.60: real thick-thin contrast. Setting it to 0.95 would make Display a wide Sans at heading sizes — cheaper to space, and it would stop Display being a separate role at all. Not built: it is a one-parameter change and building it would imply the decision was open.

A3 · Mono x-height — 0.743 vs 0.714

policy_version: v3 → 09:14Z

Mono's x-height is deliberately larger than Sans's so 12 px code stays readable. Matching Sans instead would make the two roles more obviously related and slightly harder to read in small code. Not built, for the same reason as A2.

A4 · & — redraw it

&

Current: a double-loop form that holds at display sizes and gets ambiguous at 12–14 px. Recommended: redraw this one glyph. @ and ? were already redrawn in this revision, which is why they are no longer listed here.

11 · Limitations — stated, not buried

What is not good enough yet

  • & is still idiosyncratic. Observable at 12–14 px in section 1. It is one glyph; it should be redrawn before the face is used at small UI sizes. (@ and ? were redrawn in this revision.)
  • No hinting, no print proof, no type-designer review. The fonts pass the gates; the gates measure coverage, outlines, metrics and kerning, not beauty.
  • The square dot is a decision, not a detail. It is the family's identity and the most likely thing to be rejected in UI text. The alternative is built and rendered in A1 so the choice can be made on evidence.
  • Not a variable font. Three roles × 7 static faces. A wght axis is feasible because the topology matches, and was not attempted rather than attempted and shipped unvalidated.
  • Coverage stops at 204 codepoints. No caret, pipe, ligatures, Greek, Cyrillic or CJK; those fall back to a licensed OFL face. The fallback is a degradation and it is visible when it happens.
  • Tracking and spacing are first-pass. The specimen shows occasional uneven space around r, r. and f sequences; the kerning table covers 39 pairs, not the hundreds a production face would carry.
  • The mobile panels are container-driven, the product is not. Reflow below 420 px is specified here and in the experience specimens; no product surface has been restyled to prove it.
  • Fourteen of the letters are geometric-by-construction. That is the family's premise; a reader who wants movement, modulation or history in the letterforms will not find it here.

12 · Status

Not approved, and not a substitute for the owner's judgement

Every face in this gallery is PROPOSED. The technical gates pass — coverage, outlines, metrics, kerning and byte-reproducibility are all verified against the compiled binaries — and that is precisely the point: a passing gate is not design approval. The owner rejected the current website's typography, spacing and colour. Nothing here has been accepted in place of that judgement, and no production surface has been restyled with it.
